## Translation questions still unresolved

- Final product wording should decide whether to preserve archaic Long phrasing or display a modernized paraphrase beside the historical quote.
- Epictetus's control language should be explained carefully; “up to us” is often philosophically more precise than broad modern notions of control.
- Marcus Aurelius VI.6 has small translation differences around revenge/retaliation/defense.
- Seneca Letter 13.4 varies between “imagination,” “apprehension,” and related renderings.

## Rights questions still unresolved

- International rights status has not been cleared for every translation. Current public-domain determinations are primarily U.S.-oriented.
- Musonius/Lutz remains citation-only.
- Basore 1932 should receive a second rights check before international deployment.

## Weak / rejected mappings

The research intentionally did **not** force Stoicism into every category.

Notable boundaries:
- financial fear: Stoic control teaching is only partial; acute material need requires practical support
- low mood: Seneca Letter 28 is narrow and is not treatment
- identity: Stoicism offers practice/self-examination themes but not a modern identity-development model
- betrayal: current Stoic fit concerns response to wrongdoing, not trust repair
- substance use/anhedonia: no user-facing Stoic record promoted in this phase
