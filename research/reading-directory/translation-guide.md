# Translation / Edition Guide

## General
Prefer an edition that is clearly identified. Historical public-domain translations are valuable for open access but can use dated vocabulary. Modern translations may be more readable or philologically stronger while remaining copyrighted.

## Key cautions
- **Stoicism:** Epictetus's phrase often rendered as "in our control" is better understood as what is "up to us"; avoid implying control over circumstances.
- **Early Buddhism:** historical English translations may use "self" in ways that can be confused with Hindu atman doctrine.
- **Bhagavad Gita:** Arnold is poetic rather than strictly literal. Do not treat his wording as the only translation.
- **Upanishads:** Müller is historically important but dated; individual Upanishads and later Vedanta schools should remain distinct.
- **Yoga Sutras:** sutras are extremely compressed and normally commentary-dependent.
- **Daoism:** wu wei varies across "non-action," "non-forcing," and "uncontrived action"; do not reduce it to passivity.
- **Confucian texts:** ren, li, yi, xin, and related terms resist stable one-word English equivalents.
- **Zen:** koan translations require practice/context awareness; apparent paradox is not a license for literal metaphysical claims.
