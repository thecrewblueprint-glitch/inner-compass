## 11. Compulsive / behavioral coping (v1.0 #11; old 9-split-b)

**Root mapping carried forward:** Death, Meaninglessness

### Currency check (2024–2026)

- **DSM-5-TR text updates (Sept 2024, Sept 2025)** did not add new behavioral-addiction diagnoses; gambling disorder remains the only behavioral addiction formally certified in the main text, with internet gaming disorder still parked in Section III (research criteria, not a clinical diagnosis) — this is unchanged from the first-pass finding, now confirmed current as of the most recent revision cycle.
- **ICD-11 continues to diverge from DSM-5-TR**: ICD-11 gives gaming disorder full clinical diagnostic status (6C51), and separately lists compulsive sexual behaviour disorder as an impulse-control disorder (6C72) and compulsive buying-shopping disorder as an "other specified impulse control disorder" (6C7Y) — neither of the latter two is in DSM-5-TR at all. A 2024 *American Journal of Psychiatry* review ("Current Advances in Behavioral Addictions") frames five conditions as the current clinically-relevant behavioral-addiction set: gambling disorder, internet gaming disorder, compulsive sexual behavior disorder, compulsive buying-shopping disorder, and problematic social media use — confirming the category's scope (gambling/shopping/overeating/doomscrolling) is well within current clinical thinking, even though most of these remain formally uncertified in DSM-5-TR.
- **New prevalence data**: a 2024 *Lancet Public Health* systematic review/meta-analysis puts global problem-gambling prevalence at 1.9%, and 5.3% in North America specifically — higher than the ~1.29% figure in earlier (2022) reviews. A 2025 meta-analysis found psychiatric comorbidity in 82.2% of people with gambling disorder (substance use disorders 34.2%, mood disorders 30.9%, anxiety disorders 29.9%). Online gambling participation rose from 15% (2018) to 22% (2024), tracking with increased accessibility. Compulsive buying-shopping disorder prevalence is estimated at roughly 5%.
- **Nothing changed** on the core Frankl/existential-vacuum grounding for the Meaninglessness root — this remains the strongest-evidenced link in the category and is still actively cited in 2025 sources.

### Gap-fill

The first-pass research explicitly flagged the **Death root as "more debatable... it likely reflects mortality/self-destruction risk... rather than a Yalom-style impermanence theme," calling it "defensible but worth flagging."** This pass searched specifically to resolve that flag:
- Found a published clinical case ("Gambling Disorder and suicide risk – a clinical case," PMC) and broader literature confirming gambling disorder carries measurably elevated suicide risk, which supports Death as a *mortality-risk* mapping (a real, citable clinical stake) rather than a philosophical inference.
- However, no source was found that reframes behavioral/process addiction through Terror Management Theory or impermanence-awareness the way Terror Management Theory was invoked for fear-of-failure or financial fear in the sibling categories. **Conclusion: the Death-root mapping remains a reasonable inference, now somewhat better grounded (mortality/suicide-risk data exists), but it is still not directly sourced as an existential-impermanence theme.** Guidance content should lean on mortality/loss-of-life-years framing for this root, not literal Buddhist impermanence teachings, until better source material is found.

### Real-world clinical practice grounding

- **CBT is the dominant evidence-based treatment for gambling disorder.** A 2024 meta-analysis (15 studies, 1,536 participants) found CBT produced more favorable gambling cognitions, coping behavior, and self-efficacy versus minimal/no-treatment controls, with the strongest effect on self-efficacy. AAFP's 2026 clinical review confirms CBT as first-line, sometimes combined with pharmacotherapy for higher-severity presentations.
- **Compulsive buying-shopping disorder**: CBT is the best-studied psychotherapy (Mitchell et al. 2006 was the first controlled trial); SSRIs have shown promise in open-label trials but not controlled superiority over placebo.
- **Prevalence anchors this as a real, non-fringe clinical phenomenon**: gambling disorder ~0.5% of US adults at strict clinical threshold, 1.9–5.3% at problem-gambling threshold; compulsive buying ~5%. This is squarely within the range of other well-recognized clinical conditions, supporting the category's clinical legitimacy for a philosophy-guidance app — while flagging that comorbidity (82.2%) and suicide risk mean severe presentations need a referral pathway, not just philosophical content.

### Case grounding

1. **Real case report** — "The nightmare of scratch tickets: a case report on Gambling Disorder" (PMC, [link](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC12436861/)): a 66-year-old woman with escalating scratch-ticket gambling and co-occurring depressive symptoms, illustrating the self-medication/affect-regulation pattern (Khantzian) alongside a real mortality/health-decline stake.
2. **Real case report** — "Gambling Disorder and suicide risk – a clinical case" (PMC, [link](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10661071/)): documents pathological gambling with impaired functioning, financial ruin, and elevated suicide risk — grounds the Death-root mapping in an actual clinical presentation rather than pure inference.
3. **Illustrative composite (not a documented case):** A user describes compulsively refreshing shopping apps or doomscrolling late into the night "to not think about how pointless everything feels," accumulating debt or lost sleep, unable to stop despite wanting to. This is constructed from the Khantzian self-medication framing (regulating painful affect) combined with Frankl's existential-vacuum/mass-neurotic-triad pattern (addiction as one leg of meaninglessness-driven neurosis) — it is not drawn from any single published case.

## 12. Betrayal / broken trust (v1.0 #12; old 10)

**Root mapping carried forward:** Isolation

### Currency check (2024–2026)

- **New synthesis on trust repair**: a 2025 systematic literature review in the *Journal of Family Therapy* (Giacobbi et al.) consolidated the empirical literature on post-betrayal trust repair into five concrete mechanisms: proactive transparency, active monitoring, remorse and accountability, shared activities, and clear communication of reasons for the betrayal. This is new, more operational content than the first-pass research had (which stopped at "betrayal trauma theory" and general infidelity literature).
- **New trauma-informed couples-therapy synthesis (2025, Springer)** names four mechanics of effective practice: emotional/psychological safety first, explicit trust-building norms, structured repair of ruptures, and attunement-focused (not blame-focused) conversation.
- **Active clinical-trial and theory development**: a registered clinical trial ("The Trauma of Betrayal: Treating Adjustment Disorder With Reconsolidation Blockade Under Propranolol," ClinicalTrials.gov NCT03151681) is testing a pharmacological adjunct to memory reconsolidation for betrayal-related adjustment disorder — a genuinely new (post-2024) direction not present in the original research pass. A 2024 *Journal of Sex & Marital Therapy* paper, "Betrayal Trauma Anger: Clinical Implications," extends Freyd's framework specifically to anger as a clinical presentation.
- **The Gottman Institute is running an international study validating Gottman Method Couples Therapy specifically for affair recovery** — an ongoing, not-yet-complete validation effort, meaning Gottman-based affair-recovery protocols are widely used in practice but the formal efficacy evidence base is still being built as of this writing.
- Nothing in the 2024–2026 literature contradicts the original Freyd betrayal-trauma-theory framing; it updates and operationalizes it rather than displacing it.

### Gap-fill

The first-pass research flagged: **"a case could be made for a light secondary Meaninglessness mapping (betrayal often shatters a person's narrative/assumptive-world)... this is more of a soft suggestion than something the literature strongly forces."** This pass searched specifically for support and found none that upgrades this beyond a soft suggestion — no 2024–2026 source explicitly frames betrayal through a meaning/assumptive-world-loss lens with the directness Frankl gives to addiction. **Conclusion: still an inference, not disproven but not newly supported either. Recommend keeping single-root (Isolation) as the primary mapping**, consistent with the original recommendation, and not adding Meaninglessness without further evidence.

### Real-world clinical practice grounding

- **Emotionally Focused Therapy (EFT)**, specifically the Attachment Injury Resolution Model (AIRM), is a named, structured approach for working through betrayal/affair injuries in couples therapy.
- **Gottman Method Couples Therapy** is widely used for affair recovery in current practice (international validation study underway as of 2025).
- **Trauma-informed, staged approaches** (safety → trust-norms → structured repair → attunement) are the current standard-of-care shape regardless of specific modality.
- **A real qualitative clinical study** found that of participants describing betrayal, all scored above clinical trauma thresholds; 38.5% met criteria for adjustment disorder and 61.5% met criteria for other specified trauma- and stressor-related conditions — this is strong, current (2024–2025-referenced) evidence that betrayal is not merely a folk-psychology label but produces diagnosable trauma-spectrum presentations in a meaningful minority-to-majority of cases.
- **Prevalence**: per the 2022 General Social Survey (still the most-cited recent figure), 20% of men and 13% of women report having been sexually unfaithful; broader definitions including emotional affairs push self-report rates to 45% (men) / 35% (women) (AAMFT, 2024); roughly 20–25% of married couples experience infidelity at some point; one in three Americans self-report having cheated on a partner (YouGov, 2024).

### Case grounding

1. **Cited pattern from a real study** (not a single-patient case report, but a published clinical sample): the qualitative study of betrayal narratives in which 13 participants all scored above clinical trauma thresholds, with roughly 6 in 10 meeting criteria for a trauma/stressor-related condition — grounds the "betrayal produces real trauma symptoms, not just sadness" claim in actual data rather than inference.
2. **Illustrative composite (not a documented case):** A user reports discovering a partner lied about a years-long affair; the relationship continues, but the user describes intrusive thoughts, hypervigilance about the partner's phone/whereabouts, and difficulty trusting their own perception of the relationship's history. Constructed from the trauma-symptom pattern (hyperarousal, intrusive thoughts, betrayal blindness) described across the Freyd betrayal-trauma literature and the 2025 trust-repair review — not drawn from a specific published case.
3. **Illustrative composite (not a documented case):** A user describes a business partner or close friend who lied about finances or broke a long-standing commitment; the relationship isn't romantic, but the user reports feeling unable to rely on their own judgment about who is safe to trust going forward — constructed from Freyd's institutional/extended betrayal-trauma concept, distinguishing this from category 6 (Rejection/heartbreak, where the relationship *ended*) per the documented dividing line.

## 13. General life meaninglessness (v1.0 #13; old 11-split-a)

**Root mapping carried forward:** Meaninglessness

### Currency check (2024–2026)

- **New, more precise data on the Presence-vs-Search distinction the first pass flagged**: a 2026 cross-sectional analysis from the Netherlands Study of Depression and Anxiety (NESDA) and a 2025 three-level meta-analysis both confirm that *presence* of meaning (and its inverse, "emptiness") is a significant, distinct predictor of depression, while *searching* for meaning was **not** a significant predictor in the NESDA analysis — refining and hardening the original MLQ Presence/Search distinction with newer, more direct data than the first pass had. Coherence (the ability to make logical sense of one's experience) had the strongest negative correlation with depression of any meaning sub-component identified.
- **A 2025 study specifically on adolescents** found meaning-in-life and "emptiness" both independently predict depression in that population — useful because it's a population not explicitly covered before.
- **RCT-level evidence has strengthened**: meaning-in-life-focused interventions (including ACT- and logotherapy-adjacent protocols) are now described in 2024–2025 sources as reducing not just general distress but specifically hopelessness and suicidal ideation, not previously cited in the first-pass research.
- **Age moderates the protective effect**: the presence-of-meaning-to-lower-depression link peaks in middle-aged adults and is weakest in adolescents — a genuinely new nuance not present in the original pass, relevant to how the app might calibrate content by user age if that data is ever available.
- Nothing changed on the core Yalom/Frankl grounding; it remains current and still actively cited through 2025–2026 popular-clinical writing (e.g., Psychology Today's "Crisis of Meaning in Contemporary Society," Oct 2025).

### Gap-fill

The original research for this category carried no explicit "reasonable inference, not directly sourced" flag of its own (the Meaninglessness root mapping was called "strongly supported"). The adjacent flag that does bear on this category — from `TAXONOMY_V1.0.md`'s matching notes — is the boundary caution that "nothing I do matters" should not silently absorb category 19/23's low-mood/depression cases. **This pass's search directly supports that caution being maintained, and gives it firmer footing**: presence-of-meaning and depression are now shown (2025–2026 data) to be measurably distinct predictors, not just theoretically distinct constructs — so keeping this category separate from low mood/anhedonia is now better evidenced than it was in the first pass, not merely asserted.

### Real-world clinical practice grounding

- **Logotherapy** (Frankl) remains an active, named clinical approach: guiding a client toward meaning through creative/productive engagement, experiential/relational engagement, or a chosen attitude toward unavoidable suffering. It has documented use reducing cravings and improving meaning-in-life scores in alcohol-affected populations (including partners of people with alcohol use disorder) — directly relevant to this app's adjacent category 11.
- **Acceptance and Commitment Therapy (ACT)** is the dominant modern evidence-based approach covering similar ground (values clarification, committed action) and shares substantial theoretical overlap with logotherapy; a 2025 bibliometric analysis confirms ACT research volume is still growing, with systematic reviews covering 2014–2024 RCTs across psychiatric populations.
- **This is not a standalone DSM/ICD diagnosis** — meaninglessness/"existential vacuum" is a transdiagnostic risk factor and a legitimate target of psychotherapy (logotherapy, ACT, meaning-centered psychotherapy for cancer patients, etc.) rather than a disorder itself. This matters directly for the app's scope: it is squarely "safe" territory for philosophy-guidance content, with referral relevant mainly if it co-occurs with clinical depression/suicidality (per the transdiagnostic literature above).

### Case grounding

*No single-patient published case report specific to "general meaninglessness" (as opposed to a specific disorder) was found — meaning-in-life deficit is typically studied at the population/cohort level (MLQ, WAMI, NESDA) rather than via individual case reports. Both vignettes below are therefore composites.*

1. **Illustrative composite (not a documented case):** A user with a stable job, intact relationships, and no diagnosable depression reports "nothing I do actually matters, it just goes in circles," with no anhedonia and no sad mood — constructed to match Frankl's "noogenic neurosis" pattern (meaning-domain disturbance without drive-conflict pathology) and the NESDA-era finding that presence-of-meaning deficit is measurable independent of depression.
2. **Illustrative composite (not a documented case):** An older adult nearing retirement describes a sense that their life's "story doesn't add up to anything," reflecting the "coherence" sub-component identified as most strongly (negatively) correlated with depression in the 2025 meta-analysis — again, an inference from aggregate research, not a specific published case.

## 14. Career / vocational purpose (v1.0 #14; old 11-split-b)

**Root mapping carried forward:** Meaninglessness

### Currency check (2024–2026)

- **Career construction theory** (Savickas) is confirmed as the dominant current paradigm in career counseling (2024 Frontiers review): it organizes vocational identity into *vocational personality* ("what"), *career adaptability* ("how"), and *life theme* ("why") — the "life theme"/"why" dimension maps directly onto this category's meaning-of-work framing and gives it a more current, named clinical framework than the first-pass research had (which relied mainly on Steger's WAMI).
- **A 2025 review on "career calling in the work stress process"** (*Journal of Vocational Behavior*/Tandfonline) treats a sense of vocational calling as a variable that measurably buffers or exacerbates work stress — new, directly relevant content.
- **A 2025 purpose-driven vocational intervention framework** built around "work hope" (goals, pathways, self-efficacy) is now in active clinical-trial testing for populations with co-occurring mental health/substance use conditions (a Veterans Affairs trial, ClinicalTrials.gov NCT07097376) — evidence this is an active, funded area of applied intervention research, not just theory.
- **Workplace-meaning survey data has shifted materially since the first pass**: 2024–2025 data shows only 44% of Gen Z and 46% of millennials with poor mental well-being feel their job lets them make a meaningful contribution; "quiet quitting" remains active in 2024–2025 (roughly one in three full-time employees report doing it), with "job crafting" emerging as a named counter-trend (employees proactively reshaping roles to recover meaning). This is genuinely new, current data the first pass did not have.

### Gap-fill

The original research had no "reasonable inference, not directly sourced" flag specific to this category beyond noting the WAMI/MLQ split is empirically supported (which it is). This pass's search reinforces rather than needs to resolve any gap: **career-construction theory's explicit "life theme = why" framing independently confirms, from a different research tradition than Steger's, that vocational meaning is treated as a distinct construct from general life meaning.** No unresolved inference remains for this category.

### Real-world clinical practice grounding

- **Career construction theory-based counseling** (narrative career counseling, "life-design" interviews) is current standard practice in vocational psychology, distinct from clinical psychotherapy — this category sits more in the career-counseling world than the clinical-disorder world, which is directly useful for the app's scope: it confirms this is safe, non-clinical territory for philosophy-guidance content (dharma/karma-yoga framing), with no diagnosis or crisis threshold typically implicated.
- **Work-hope/vocational-identity interventions** are used clinically for populations where vocational purpose loss intersects with mental illness or substance use (the VA trial above), showing this category can co-occur with more clinical presentations (category 10/11) without itself being a clinical disorder.
- **Prevalence-adjacent data**: no formal "disorder" prevalence exists (there is none — this isn't a diagnosis), but survey data shows meaningful-work deficits are widespread: under half of younger workers with poor well-being report their job feels meaningful, and roughly a third of full-time employees report actively disengaging (quiet quitting) as of 2024.

### Case grounding

*No published clinical case reports exist for "career purpose confusion" as such, since it is not a diagnosis — vocational-meaning research is survey/cohort-based, not case-report-based. Both vignettes are composites.*

1. **Illustrative composite (not a documented case):** A user who has been promoted twice and is financially secure reports "I'm good at my job and I still don't see the point of any of it," while describing strong meaning in their personal relationships — constructed to match the WAMI/career-construction-theory finding that general life meaning and work-specific meaning are separately measured constructs.
2. **Illustrative composite (not a documented case):** A younger user describes doing "the bare minimum" at work, disengaged but not job-searching, framing it as "why would I give more when none of it matters to anyone" — constructed from the 2024–2025 quiet-quitting/meaningful-contribution survey data (not a specific published case).

## 15. Overwhelm / too much to control (v1.0 #15; old 12)

**Root mapping carried forward:** Freedom

### Currency check (2024–2026)

- **ICD-11's burnout classification is unchanged but its clinical boundaries are important and were under-emphasized in the first pass**: burnout is classified strictly as an "occupational phenomenon" (a factor influencing health status), explicitly *not* a medical diagnosis, and the WHO definition explicitly states it "should not be applied to describe experiences in other areas of life." This is a meaningful currency/gap-fill finding: the first-pass research cited Maslach's burnout model as central evidence for this category, but category 15 as taxonomized ("too much to control," not "workplace burnout") is broader than what burnout research strictly covers.
- **New prevalence data (2024–2025) shows burnout is worse than in most earlier datasets**: Gallup 2024 found 41% of employees experience "a lot of stress" and 49% of US/Canada workers report daily work-related stress; a November 2025 Eagle Hill Consulting survey found 55% of the US workforce currently experiencing burnout — a six-year high; other 2025 surveys report figures from 43% to 76% depending on instrument and definition (there is no single validated diagnostic threshold, consistent with burnout's non-diagnosis status).
- **An active, unresolved 2024–2025 scientific debate** (Bianchi et al. on one side; De Beer, Schaufeli, Demerouti, Bakker, Leiter and colleagues on the other) contests whether burnout is a distinct construct or a depression subtype/manifestation — this is new information the first pass did not have, and it is directly relevant to how confidently the app can treat "overwhelm" as separate from low mood (category 23). Distinguishing clinical features that have emerged: burnout tends to improve with time away from the stressor/context change, while depression persists across contexts; anhedonia is a depression-specific marker not typically present in burnout.
- **New intervention evidence**: a 2025 meta-analysis (*Annals of Internal Medicine*, 93 RCTs, 9,330 participants) found individual-level mindfulness and yoga programs produced the most consistent improvements in exhaustion and depersonalization, with workshop-based approaches showing smaller effects; separately, participatory organizational-redesign interventions (workload/team-support changes) show the strongest evidence for *sustained* (12-month) burnout reduction — evidence organizational-level, not just individual-level, intervention matters, which the first pass did not cover.

### Gap-fill

The first-pass research flagged the mapping as "unchanged — supported, with a caveat below," with the caveat being overlap risk with categories 3 and 7. **This pass surfaces a sharper, previously under-flagged gap: the strongest clinical evidence base (Maslach's burnout model, ICD-11) is specifically occupational, while the taxonomy category is written broadly ("too much to control," not limited to work).** The category's non-workplace presentations (a parent overwhelmed by caregiving, a student overwhelmed by demands) are better and more directly grounded in the *locus-of-control* (Rotter) and *allostatic-load* literature already cited in the first pass than in burnout research specifically — this should be treated as a confirmed, not merely inferred, distinction going forward: burnout material is a strong sub-case of this category (with real clinical/prevalence weight) but should not be assumed to cover every "overwhelm" presentation the matching layer will see.

### Real-world clinical practice grounding

- **For workplace-driven overwhelm/burnout**: organizational-level interventions (participatory workload redesign, team-support restructuring) currently have the strongest evidence for sustained reduction; individual-level mindfulness- and yoga-based programs have the most consistent evidence among individual interventions (2025 meta-analysis, 93 RCTs).
- **For broader control-appraisal overwhelm** (not workplace-specific): CBT-style interventions targeting locus-of-control and perceived-control appraisals, and stress-management approaches addressing unpredictability/low perceived control (per the allostatic-load research already cited), are the applicable evidence base.
- **Prevalence**: workplace burnout specifically now affects an estimated 43–55%+ of the US workforce by various 2024–2025 measures (no single gold-standard instrument), confirming this is a widespread, real phenomenon — while the *diagnostic* status remains deliberately non-medical (ICD-11 "occupational phenomenon," not a disorder), which is a useful design signal: this category is largely appropriate for philosophy-guidance content, with referral relevant mainly when burnout-pattern language co-occurs with anhedonia or persists regardless of context change (signals suggesting clinical depression rather than burnout per the 2024–2025 differentiation literature).

### Case grounding

1. **Illustrative composite (not a documented case):** A hospital nurse describes chronic exhaustion, growing cynicism toward patients they used to care about deeply, and a sense that nothing they do makes a difference anymore, but reports genuine relief and re-engagement during a two-week vacation — constructed directly from Maslach's three-dimension burnout model (exhaustion, cynicism/depersonalization, reduced efficacy) and the 2024–2025 differentiation literature's "improves with context change" burnout marker (distinguishing it from depression).
2. **Illustrative composite (not a documented case):** A parent of three managing a job, caregiving, and a family member's illness describes "everything is on fire at once and none of it is really in my control," with no single workplace driver — constructed from Rotter's locus-of-control literature and allostatic-load research (unpredictability/low perceived control driving the "too much" response) rather than burnout research specifically, illustrating why this category should not be treated as workplace-burnout-only.
